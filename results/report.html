What changed the plan

The first "CUDA" run was secretly on CPU

The newest cuDNN (9.25) fails on this driver at the very first Conv node, and onnxruntime quietly rebuilds the session on CPU and carries on. Every mode came out at the same speed (RTFx 13) and that was the tell. Fixed by pinning cuDNN 9.1.1 with the CUDA 12.4 runtimes, disabling the fallback so it is a hard error, and re-checking providers after warm-up. Driver 551.23 also rules out CUDA 13, so onnxruntime-gpu is pinned to 1.26 and torch to 2.6 cu124. A driver update to 580 or newer lifts all of these pins.

The decoder belongs on the CPU

The transducer decoder runs once per 80 ms frame with tiny tensors. On the GPU each of those calls is launch-bound (0.5 to 0.7 ms per frame); the int8 decoder on CPU runs at 0.14 to 0.24 ms per frame. The encoder on CUDA takes 30 to 40 ms even for a minute of audio. "Hybrid" (CUDA encoder, int8 CPU decoder) is 1.4× faster than all-GPU on short clips and 2.7× on the monologue, and is now the default.

Accuracy equals Handy's, plus fillers and punctuation

Every difference from Handy's transcript of the same audio is a filler word. Handy strips "um" and "uh"; Parakeet keeps them and adds capitals and punctuation. No real word errors on the five files. The word-error figures against Handy (0.05 to 0.15) are entirely those fillers.

Windows renumbers audio devices between sessions

Index 52 was the Yeti when enumerated during the build and a virtual cable when you ran it, so the first live attempts heard silence. The scripts now select the mic by name (--device Yeti) and print which device they opened.

Silero VAD: your pauses are 0.3 to 0.6 s

Silero costs 0.58 ms per 32 ms chunk (p95 0.70 ms) on CPU and catches the first word 100 to 400 ms after onset. The hangover, the silence it waits for before declaring the end, decides whether one thought becomes one utterance or five.

hangoversegments in the 64 s monologuewhat split it
300 ms11every 0.3 to 0.6 s thinking pause
500 ms3two 0.6 s pauses
800 ms1nothing
1200 ms1nothing

A silence-only endpointer therefore needs 600 to 800 ms of hangover for the way you talk, and pays it on every turn. That is the case for a turn model.

Smart Turn v3.2: it knows a pause from an ending

Pipecat's 8.7 MB end-of-turn model, run on CPU with onnxruntime, listens to the last 8 s of waveform and scores whether you sound finished. All five whole recordings scored done (0.70 to 0.99). The real test is the monologue cut at each of Silero's 300 ms pauses, the exact moments a silence-only endpointer would have interrupted you.

The repeated "it is" is in the recording: three different decoder configurations transcribe it identically, so it is not a decoder loop. ASR inside the loop is 3 to 4× slower than offline on the same audio (100 to 240 ms against 45 to 79 ms). The likely cause is the torch VAD loop and the int8 CPU decoder sharing one Python process. Moving Silero to onnxruntime or the ASR to its own process is the next experiment.

Kokoro-82M: the other half of the turn

Kokoro renders a whole segment at once, so the streaming that matters is pipelining by sentence: LLM tokens pass through a sentence chunker, a generator thread renders sentence N+1 while sentence N plays, and interrupt() flushes the queue for barge-in. The agent: barge-in, tools, live partials

agent/talk.py is the conversational loop on LangGraph with Claude Haiku 4.5. An explicit state machine (listening, user speaking, thinking, speaking, interrupted) owns every transition; an echo gate cross-correlates each mic chunk with the last second the speaker played so the agent cannot interrupt itself; Parakeet re-decodes the growing utterance every 0.6 s so your words appear while you are still saying them. After a barge-in the history holds only what you actually heard, plus a note of what was cut, so "go on" continues from the right place.

Recommendations

  • Budget: about 50 ms of ASR per utterance, 150 ms per minute of speech, 3.6 GB of VRAM, in hybrid mode.
  • Endpointing: 600 ms hangover on Silero alone; about 300 ms plus a 70 ms Smart Turn check with the turn model.
  • Pre-roll: keep the 1 s ring buffer; onset detection lags 100 to 400 ms.
  • Whole turn: speech end → text ~0.8 s (≈0.5 s with Smart Turn) + the model's first token + ~0.45 s to first Kokoro audio. GPU: 3.6 GB Parakeet + 0.55 GB Kokoro, both resident.
  • Driver: update NVIDIA to 580 or newer when convenient; that removes the CUDA 12, cuDNN and torch pins.
  • Word boosting: not available in onnx-asr. A post-hoc term corrector or NeMo on Linux/WSL would be needed.
